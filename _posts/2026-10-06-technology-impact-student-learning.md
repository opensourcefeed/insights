Technology has become a living, breathing part of learning in schools and colleges.

The shift from a whiteboard to a projector screen has brought many benefits, but also introduced a few challenges on a student's learning journey.

This is all thanks to the development and implementation of educational technology in the post-COVID era, which has had profound effects on learning beyond the classroom.

This blog will examine the definitive impact of technology on student learning, taking its history, the present, and the future into consideration.

**TL; DR**

- Technology has revolutionized learning through online platforms for learning, engagement, and assessment.
- Gutenberg's press democratized learning, and through the centuries, radio, projectors, and interactive lessons became the norm.
- Generative learning, VR, AR, and cloud technology are on the rise for improved student learning.
- Positive impacts include easy access, improved engagement, easy collaboration, and tailored lessons.
- Educational technology is also fueling distractions, overreliance on tools, and privacy problems.

## Technology in Education - A Historical Perspective

Johannes Gutenberg invented the first movable press in Europe and spearheaded the publishing of textbooks and scriptures. Throughout the centuries, inventions found their way into classrooms to aid students in learning.

In the past century, radio and projectors became staples in American classrooms. At the peak of the Cold War, the US passed the Vocational Education Act that paved the way for early computer literacy and application.

Digital learning became ubiquitous at the turn of the century with online courses and digital content, and affordable [internet plans](https://www.localcabledeals.com/ca/los-angeles){:rel="sponsored"} making digital content easy to access for students.

## Positive Impact of Technology on Student Learning

Technology has revolutionized student learning in a way that modern classrooms are unrecognizable from those of the past. Manual delivery and mindless cramming have been replaced with interactive and engaging lessons, helping teachers get the message across loud and clear.

### Access to Resources

Technology has completely revamped the way students learn and grow academically.

The development of online academic resources, such as online libraries, eBooks, video courses, and podcasts, has made it easier to finish lessons. That's why around 75% of online graduates believe online education is better, or at least equal to, in-person learning, according to a [BestColleges survey](https://www.bestcolleges.com/research/online-learning-statistics/).

To better access these resources, you need consistent high-speed internet. Thankfully, platforms like LocalCableDeals have made it easy, helping customers compare providers and pick the right internet provider for your home.

### Better Student Engagement

Before, the mode of lesson delivery was just in the classroom, in a physical classroom.

Now, technology has broadened the horizons with interactive materials, like quizzes, games, and simulations, to make lessons interesting and easy to understand.

These modes are also good at letting students study at their own pace, even further exploring topics and subjects they find interesting.

Additionally, discussion boards and online collaboration tools let students engage with teachers and peers, transcending theory into practice.

### Enhanced Collaboration

Collaboration was a missed opportunity in the pre-technology era.

Technology offers prompt and flexible tools to collaborate through emails, messages, and Learning Management Systems (LMS). Students can ask a question and clear a doubt without waiting for a specific window.

Tools like Google Docs and Zoom let students build projects and edit papers from the comfort of their homes.

The superpower of live and delayed communication, with video calls and comments, further promotes collaboration and group efforts.

### Custom Lessons and Immediate Feedback

AI and generative technology have allowed educators to customize lessons to meet the needs of their students.

Whether you are a visual, auditory, or tactile learner, custom lessons ensure you get your lesson tailored around your needs and preferences. Moreover, assistive technologies like screen readers can help students with learning disabilities and challenges.

On the other hand, smart technology offers prompt feedback for students. This helps them determine their position and work on weaker areas without wasting time and effort.

## Challenges with Educational Technology

The challenges arising from the use of technology in the educational space are few, but they are here. Here is a snapshot of negative aspects of technology in student learning:

- **Reduced Focus:** Learning, gaming, and scrolling social media on the same device can result in less time learning and [reduced attention span](https://www.apa.org/news/podcasts/speaking-of-psychology/attention-spans).
- **Digital Divide:** Many students in rural and low-income families do not have access to the materials that their peers might have, fueling the digital divide.
- **Overreliance:** Overreliance on the latest technology leaves little incentive for students to engage in personal decision-making, critical thinking, and problem-solving capabilities.
- **Privacy and Security:** Collection of students' personal data can leave them vulnerable to tracking and potential data breaches.

## Future of Technology in the Classroom

As mentioned earlier, the adoption of technology in student learning is an ongoing process. With time, new technologies are emerging and getting feedback from educators and students to suit their needs.

The future looks promising as Artificial Intelligence, Virtual Reality, and other cutting-edge technologies are making learning easier for students while resolving issues. From personalized learning to easy, quick feedback, technology will be at the helm of teaching.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does technology enable students with special needs?

Technology offers assistive tools like screen readers, text-to-speech, and other aids to make sure students with special needs are not left behind.

### What are some risks of using technology in education?

There are some risks in using technology for student learning, such as data misuse, breaches, and overreliance on tools, resulting in unwanted issues.

### Does technology in education make teachers obsolete?

Technology does not make teachers obsolete but helps them provide students with custom lessons, quick feedback, and improved engagement.
