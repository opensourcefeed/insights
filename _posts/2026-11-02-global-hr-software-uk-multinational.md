## What global HR software means for a UK buyer

Global HR software is the system that manages employees and contractors across countries from one place. Depending on the product, that spans employee records, onboarding, absence, performance, reporting, and pay. Some of these tools are core HR platforms, also called an HRIS: they hold the workforce your own entities already employ and run your HR processes on top of that. Others are global-payroll or employer-of-record services that carry the legal and payroll mechanics of workers in markets where you have no company. The words on the homepage often look identical, so a UK buyer has to read what each product does rather than what it's called.

### Core HR versus an employer of record

One is a piece of software, the other is a legal employer. Your core HR platform is the software: it manages records, onboarding, absence, performance, and pay for the people your entities employ, and it never signs as anyone's employer. An employer of record signs. It puts its name on the local contract, runs the payroll, and shoulders the statutory duties in a country where you've not registered, while your managers still direct the work. The line blurred because global-payroll platforms bolted on HR features and core HR platforms added international payroll, so plenty of UK teams shortlist one when their real need calls for the other. Settle your requirement first, then read the list.

### The UK compliance baseline

For a UK-headquartered company, the local anchor comes before any global feature matters. HMRC-recognised payroll with RTI submissions (FPS and EPS), auto-enrolment pensions, statutory sick and parental pay, and P60 and P45 handling all have to work at home, under GDPR and UK data-protection rules and the day-one rights the Employment Rights Act 2025 phases in, before you extend the same record abroad. A platform that runs native UK payroll and holds employee data to GDPR standards gives you a compliant base to grow from. One that treats UK statutory detail as an afterthought will drain hours your team can't afford the moment the overseas entities switch on.

## Where employers of record fit beside global core HR

Search for global HR software and four names dominate the results: Deel, Remote, Oyster, and Papaya Global. Each belongs to a separate category, employer of record and global-payroll specialists, and the distinction is worth stating in plain terms. An EOR becomes the legal employer of a worker in a country where your business has no registered entity, carrying the contract, the payroll, and the statutory duties while your managers set the work. That clears the path into a new market, the very first hire before a company exists on paper. What it doesn't give you is a master record for the staff your own entities already employ. Papaya Global sits at the payroll end of this same group, sharp on cross-border pay visibility and thin on core HR, with reviewers flagging invoicing problems and delays. Treat the three profiles that follow as the entry ramp into a fresh market, not the place your wider workforce settles once the entity is live.

### Deel

![The Deel homepage showing its employer of record platform](/insights/assets/images/post-images/global-hr/global-hr-software-uk-multinational-deel.webp)

**Ideal for:** Putting staff on compliant local pay where your business has no entity.

Deel started in global hiring and widened into a sprawling workforce platform, and buyers turn to it to drop a worker onto compliant local pay at speed, in well over 100 countries, with no local company to register first. For a rapid landing in a new market, the coverage delivers. At its core Deel remains an employer of record, so it has expanded beyond EOR into a global HR platform with its own HRIS, allowing companies to keep employees, contractors, and EOR workers in one system of record. Its EOR services solve the legal-employment problem in countries where you lack an entity, although some providers, including Deel, now also offer broader HRIS capabilities for directly employed staff. Its G2 mark reads 4.7 from about 6,596 reviews, among the deeper bases here, with repeated mentions of payment snags, a bill that grows with headcount, and processing lag.

**Feature highlights:**

- EOR hiring in well over 100 countries

- Contractor pay across many currencies

- HR and integration tools stacked on the employment core

**What it costs:** Lists EOR pricing from about $599 per employee per month, alongside lighter HR and per-contractor tiers.

### Remote

![The Remote homepage showing its employer of record service](/insights/assets/images/post-images/global-hr/global-hr-software-uk-multinational-remote.webp)

**Ideal for:** Close control of local employment through entities the provider owns.

Remote chases the same market as Deel from a different footing, running its own legal entities in each country, which suits teams that want their EOR to carry the local employment tie from first day to last. The market entry stays tidy, and Remote holds its EOR role, sitting beside your core HR platform, not taking its place. A group operating its own entities still owns the record for everyone Remote isn't employing on its behalf. Remote's G2 rating is 4.5 from about 4,869 reviews, and the logged pain points gather around slow support, a feature set that trails bigger suites, and pay that arrives late.

**Feature highlights:**

- Provider-owned entities for firm control of local employment

- EOR and contractor cover across many countries

- Global payroll built into the employment platform

**What it costs:** A scoped quote by country and headcount.

### Oyster

![The Oyster homepage showing its employer of record service](/insights/assets/images/post-images/global-hr/global-hr-software-uk-multinational-oyster.webp)

**Ideal for:** Employing distributed talent without opening entities of your own.

Oyster is an employer of record aimed at distributed hiring, taking on the compliance and pay machinery of employing people across many markets without a business planting a local flag. A startup piecing together a cross-border team clears a genuine hurdle with it. Its boundary is structural, not a checklist gap: an EOR is a layer for hiring and employing, so a company that wants its people data in one place still needs a core record sitting under it, and a growing group moves past the EOR-only route as it opens entities market by market. Contractor management runs at a modest monthly per-head rate, while EOR work starts from a quote and a refundable deposit.

**Feature highlights:**

- Compliant EOR employment across a wide range of markets

- Contractor management that opens with a trial period

- Benefits and onboarding run on the platform

**What it costs:** An EOR quote plus a refundable deposit; contractor management near $29 per contractor per month.

## Which global HR software fits a UK or multinational team

Picking the best global HR software for a UK or multinational team turns on which problem you're solving, and on holding the line between the two categories. If the job is putting people to work in a country where your business has no entity, an employer of record such as Deel, Remote, or Oyster is the on-ramp built for it. If the job is holding one record for the entities already under your name, with local pay connected rather than ripped out, that's a core HR call.

For a UK company pushing into new countries, Bob suits the modern version of that job. It keeps people data joined across entities, plugs each market's existing payroll provider into the Payroll Hub, and sets the foundation on GDPR-aligned data controls and HMRC-recognised UK pay. Legacy suites trade usability for depth, and the small-business tools stop at the border. A single record your teams keep current from London to Lisbon is what separates HR that leads the 2026 expansion from HR that mops up behind it.
